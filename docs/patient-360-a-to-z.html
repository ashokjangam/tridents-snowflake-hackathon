Patient 360: the complete A to Z guide

A member chart in Snowflake that answers a question only when it can point at the exact warehouse row or document cell behind the answer, and refuses otherwise. It joins 108 simulated Synthea patients across their encounters, claims and their C-CDA clinical documents on one checked key, and is deployed as a Streamlit-in-Snowflake app built with the CoCo CLI.

1 · Why Patient 360 beats Problem 3 (Risk, fraud and regulatory) for a judge

Before writing any code we inspected all four candidate tracks against the same five 20-point dimensions. For Problem 3 we inspected the IBM AML HI-Small dataset (5,078,345 transactions); for Patient 360, the MITRE Synthea sample (CSV plus C-CDA). These are the achievable scores for a 36-hour build:

■ Patient 360: 64 achievable / 68 ceiling■ Problem 3, risk and fraud: 50 achievable / 57 ceilingSupply chain 54.5 / 61.5 · Customer 360 40 / 45

Patient 360 gives a judge

  • A checkable answer. Every sentence carries a tuple the judge can paste into SQL and see the same row.
  • Real unstructured text. 108 C-CDA documents parsed into cells and joined to the CSV chart on the same UUID, 108 of 108.
  • A question-to-finding workflow. Pick a member, read the cited chart, ask a question, get a cited answer or a refusal.
  • Governance you can see. SSN, driver's licence and passport never leave RAW; the analyst role is denied them.
  • A light build. 18 small CSVs plus 108 XML files on an X-Small warehouse, which is why the demo is finished and tested.

Problem 3 would give a judge

  • A generator tag, not a verdict. Is Laundering comes from the simulator (Altman et al.), not from an investigator decision or a filed suspicious-activity report.
  • No regulatory text. No policy passage, liquidity figure, credit outcome or filing exists on those entity ids, so a "regulatory" answer has nothing to cite.
  • Thin entities. Names are role labels such as Sole Proprietorship #50438; 1,968 of 5,177 positives have no typology row.
  • Leakage traps. File order is not time order, the post-window positive rate is 59.1%, and bank ids are zero-padded in one file only.
  • A heavy build. 5.1 million rows and a pattern parser eat the time a judge-facing app needs.

The statement permits clinical or regulatory documents; this implementation deliberately chooses the clinical C-CDA path. The primary outcome is broad cited evidence retrieval. 6 · The citation contract

An answer is allowed only if it can return one of three tuples. No tuple, no answer.

Document

(document_id, section_loinc, element_id)

A cell in a C-CDA section. Element ids repeat across files (medications-desc-1 appears in 105 of 108), so the document id is mandatory.

Table

(table, patient_id, encounter_id, code, start)

Several Synthea tables have no row id, so the natural key is patient, encounter, code and start.

Cohort

(RISK_SCORE, index_date, horizon_end, score)

A point-count bucket, with its index date and horizon, never a probability.

Why a judge cares. Each chip is a lookup they can run. The answer text is generated from the rows, so it never says anything the rows do not.

8 · The honest point count

Four points, fixed before the rates were read: age at index ≥ 65; a prior emergency or inpatient encounter; at least 8 conditions active at the index; last prior HbA1c (4548-4) ≥ 6.5. Index 2023-01-01, horizon to 2024-01-01. Outcome: any emergency or inpatient encounter in the horizon.

What we say on screen. Score 2 sits below score 1, and score 3 is 6 patients. Fourteen events cannot validate a model or support a holdout. Retuning the points after reading this table would fit the same 97 patients, so we did not. Score ≥ 2 is 41 patients at 17.07%. Seven patients who died and four born after the index are excluded.

10 · The judge-facing app

Top of the page: the population

  • One lead line: "Clinical document copilot: unified synthetic chart, claims, coverage, and C-CDA evidence."
  • Three outcome cards: clinical evidence retrieval, member-and-claims 360, guarded answers.
  • Four population metrics: 108 members, 5,571 encounters, 9,421 claims on an encounter, 108 documents matched.

Below: this member

  • Member selector (defaults to the worked patient), profile and six chart counts.
  • A one-line join: patient → encounter → claim → document.
  • Prefilled cited answers for member/encounters, conditions, care plans, labs, claims, medication and allergy evidence.
  • Each answer shows citation chips and a "Rows behind this answer" expander.
  • Example prompts also cover medication lists, procedures, immunizations and coverage spans.
  • The non-monotonic point count is below the clinical workflow as a secondary descriptive cohort audit.
Design rule. A judge who never opens an expander still sees the outcome, the evidence chips and a refusal. Raw tables are there to verify, not to read first.

14 · What Patient 360 does NOT do

  • It is not a validated risk model. Fourteen events cannot support one.
  • It does not recommend treatment, change doses or write back to the chart.
  • It does not invent notes, member ids or openFDA joins.
  • It does not train or retune anything on the 97-patient cohort.
  • It does not treat the lifetime C-CDA as an independent discharge summary.
  • It is not for care. Every record is a simulation.
