Concordia: the complete A to Z guide

Concordia is a supply chain app that runs entirely inside Snowflake. Five teams ask questions about the same business and every team gets the same number for the same question, with the definition, the coverage and the evidence attached. This guide explains, in plain English, what every screen shows, where every number comes from and what it does not prove.

Figures are from Snowflake database CONCORDIA on account fmxjowh-brc04642, read on 2 October 2026 after both data batches were loaded (publish run with 126,073 source records and 43,948 governed results). All data is synthetic.

1 · The problem

The everyday version

A VP asks: "What was our on-time delivery in May?" Five teams answer with five numbers, because each counts differently:

  • Procurement counts supplier deliveries against the purchase order date.
  • Logistics counts shipments leaving the dock.
  • Customer service counts against the latest revised promise, which flatters the number.
  • Planning uses last night's stock snapshot with different part numbers.
  • Finance books missing freight as zero, which makes cost look lower than it is.

Meetings start by arguing about whose number is right. Numbers move after month end with no record of why. A chatbot on raw tables just invents a sixth answer.

What Concordia does about it

  • One definition per metric, written down, versioned and hashed, with an owner.
  • One calculation, done once in Snowflake SQL. The app, the chat and the semantic view all read the same result.
  • Refuses to guess. "OTD" alone asks inbound or outbound. "OTIF" is refused because it is a different metric. Missing cost stays "incomplete", never zero.
  • Remembers what was known when. You can see the May number as it looked on 5 June and as it looks after late documents arrived.
  • Shows its evidence. Every answer carries the counts, the coverage, the reasons and an evidence ID.

2 · The pretend company SYNTHETIC

Meridian Motion makes servo motors. The simulator (sim/engine.py, contract CONCORDIA_SIM_V1, fixed seed) plays out every day from 1 January 2025 to 15 July 2026: orders, purchases, production, stock moves, shipments, deliveries, returns, invoices and freight bills.

Places

Plants: Dayton, Reno, Stuttgart.
Distribution centres: Newark, Oakland, Hamburg.
Customer regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, West, DACH, Benelux.

Things and parties

200 items, of which 40 are finished motors (MM-401 to MM-440).
40 suppliers, 24 customers.
The hero part is MM-440, bought finished from supplier Apex into Dayton.

Two deliveries of data

Batch 1 holds everything visible up to 5 June 2026.
Batch 2 adds the late documents: freight and brokerage bills, late proofs of delivery and June shipments. It arrives later and restates some answers.

3 · How data flows

  1. Land. Extracts are copied into LAND.RAW_RECORD exactly as received, with a hash so duplicates are ignored. Nothing is edited in place.
  2. Resolve. CORE.RESOLVE() maps each system's codes to one canonical part, party and facility, converts units and time zones, and quarantines what it cannot trust.
  3. Compute. Five SQL functions, one per metric, calculate the answer for any scope, month and "known when" date. They are the only numeric authority.
  4. Publish. APP.PUBLISH_RESULTS() runs those functions across the grid (about 41,000 answers) so the app is fast, and keeps the previous run so changes can be shown.
  5. Serve. The app and the chat read the APP layer only, through functions that apply persona rules.
  6. Automate. When new rows land, the stream-gated task CORE.RESOLVE_TASK resolves and republishes. That is how batch 2 was loaded.

4 · The five metrics GOVERNED SQL

Each metric has a written contract you can read on the Governance page. Here is what each one means, with the May 2026 MM-440 numbers you will see in the app.

Inbound supplier OTD · Procurement
Question
Did the supplier deliver the full accepted quantity of each purchase order line by the date they committed to?
Counted when
On the supplier's commitment date: their acknowledged promise if it arrived within two business days of the order, otherwise the date the buyer asked for.
Rule of thumb
A partial delivery is a miss. A rejected batch does not count as delivered.
MM-440, Dayton, May
2 of 3 lines on time = 66.7%. One line was short because 20 units were rejected.
Outbound customer OTD · Logistics
Question
Did the customer receive the complete quantity by the original promise date?
Counted when
On the original accepted promise date. Later revisions do not move the goalposts.
Rule of thumb
"Delivered" means proof of delivery (or ready for pickup when the customer collects). Leaving our dock is not enough.
MM-440, May
1 of 4 lines on time = 25.0%, down from 75–100% in earlier months. Three customer lines in the Northeast were late, partial or revised.
Unit fill rate · Logistics
Question
What share of ordered units actually shipped, regardless of timing?
Rule of thumb
Counts units, not lines. Over-shipping is capped at the ordered quantity.
MM-440, May
40 of 40 units = 100%. Everything shipped. It just arrived late, which is why OTD and fill rate disagree on purpose.
Days of inventory · Planning
Question
How many days would available stock cover recent demand?
Formula
(on hand − quality hold − allocated) × 28 ÷ demand over the last 28 days
Rule of thumb
It is a snapshot on a date, not a monthly total. Published for every finished motor represented in inventory, at site and network scope.
On the card
The value in days, with available units × 28 as the numerator and 28-day demand as the denominator.
Landed cost per accepted unit · Finance
Question
What did each accepted unit really cost to get here, including merchandise, freight, duty, insurance and brokerage, less credits?
Rule of thumb
If any required cost is missing, the answer is INCOMPLETE and no headline number is shown. Missing is never treated as zero.
MM-440, Dayton, May
As known on 5 June: INCOMPLETE (freight and brokerage bills not in yet, coverage 0%). As known on 15 July: $12.50 per unit (237,500 cents ÷ 190 units, coverage 100%).
Why the network landed cost is always INCOMPLETE. Across all parts, some receipt in every month is missing a cost document, so the network total is withheld by contract. Pick a single part and facility to see a value. This is the system refusing to guess, not a bug.

8 · How one answer is made

  1. Semantic view first. The primary mode sends the question to Cortex Analyst over APP.SUPPLY_CHAIN_ONTOLOGY. LANGUAGE MODEL
  2. Verified queries. Approved questions and their SQL live on the semantic view. If generated SQL fails, a close approved question can provide deterministic fallback SQL.
  3. SQL guard. APP.RUN_SEMANTIC_SQL allows only read-only queries over that semantic view, rejects outer arithmetic or re-aggregation, and requires metric, month, as-of and scope.
  4. Exact-scope proof. Every metric result must equal the published row for the same metric, month, known-as-of and scope. GOVERNED SQL
  5. Masking policy. Landed-cost columns are masked by Snowflake for personas without cost visibility.
  6. Evidence mode. The optional Metric evidence envelope mode calls APP.ASK_METRIC for a deterministic single-metric envelope and audit record.
Plain English. The AI is allowed to understand the question and to word the answer. It is never allowed to calculate. If it tries to change a number, even by rounding, its sentence is thrown away.

9 · As-of and restatement

Every published answer is stamped with the moment it represents. There are 19 such moments: the 5th of the month after each of 18 months (the "early" view), plus 15 July 2026 (the "final" view). Each SQL function only uses records that were visible at that moment.

Plain English. A number can change for two honest reasons: later information arrived, or a definition changed. Concordia keeps both runs and shows which numbers moved, so nobody has to wonder whether someone edited the spreadsheet.

10 · Security and governance

  • Separate roles. CONCORDIA_ADMIN builds, CONCORDIA_TRANSFORM runs the task, CONCORDIA_APP_OWNER owns the app. ACCOUNTADMIN does not own the app.
  • The app cannot read raw data. It uses selected APP secure views/functions plus the narrow governance context required for persona-aware execution; it has no direct LAND or CORE table access.
  • Masking in Snowflake. A masking policy on semantic result columns hides landed cost for Planning and Logistics. The deterministic APP functions also enforce cost entitlement.
  • Contracts are hashed. Each metric's definition has a SHA-2 hash shown on every answer, so a silent definition change would be visible.
  • Provenance is enforced. Every raw record is labelled SYNTHETIC_* and every result DERIVED_FROM_SYNTHETIC. The label OBSERVED is forbidden by tests.

12 · Five-minute demo script

  1. Command center, as Nora Hale. "Same part, same month, five teams, one card each." Point at 2/3, 1/4, 40/40 and the coverage line. Show the May dip on the trend.

13 · What this project does NOT do

  • It does not use or claim real company, customer, supplier or industry data. Every number is synthetic.
  • It does not prove time saved, accuracy on real ERP data, or any business outcome. None of that was measured.
  • It does not map personas to corporate SSO users. The app persona is selected in the UI; real Snowflake persona roles independently prove access and metric identity.
  • The primary Ask mode uses Cortex Analyst over APP.SUPPLY_CHAIN_ONTOLOGY. Cortex Search and Cortex Agents are not deployed.
  • IoT readings and lots are synthetic dock telemetry, not a live device connector.
  • It does not forecast, optimise or recommend. It answers governed questions about what happened.
  • Answers take 10–20 seconds because of the language model call.
  • The model's narration can occasionally over-explain (for example calling COMPLETE "verified"). The numbers in it are always checked.
  • MM-440's landed cost in earlier months is about $23–33 per unit versus $12.50 for the May hero receipts, which come from a fixed test fixture. Don't present that as a real price drop.

16 · Glossary

OTDOn-time delivery. Concordia has two, inbound (suppliers) and outbound (customers), and never blends them.
OTIFOn time in full. A different metric that Concordia does not define, so it refuses the term.
Fill rateShare of ordered units that shipped, ignoring timing.
Landed costEverything it costs to get a unit into your facility: price, freight, duty, insurance, brokerage, less credits.
As-of / known whenThe moment an answer represents. Only records visible by then are used.
RestatementA published answer that changed because later information arrived.
CoverageShare of the scope whose required inputs were present.
OntologyThe agreed list of business things (part, party, facility, region, order …) and how they relate.
Identity resolutionWorking out that the ERP material number, the MES item code and the WMS SKU for a motor are the same part. The One graph page shows the real codes for each part.
QuarantineRecords set aside because they cannot be trusted, instead of being silently fixed.
Metric contractThe written, versioned definition of a metric plus the one SQL function that computes it.
Evidence envelopeThe JSON record behind an answer: inputs, counts, reasons, coverage and sample records.
PersonaA demo identity whose rules decide what you can see.
Stream / TaskSnowflake features: a stream tracks new rows, a task runs a procedure when the stream has data.
Cortex COMPLETESnowflake's built-in language model call, used here only for routing and wording.
Semantic viewA Snowflake object describing tables, dimensions and metrics in business terms.
Streamlit in SnowflakePython web apps hosted inside Snowflake, using its security and warehouses.
